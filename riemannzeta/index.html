Zeros on the
critical line.

A Lean-checked proof puts a lower bound of 83.625% on the share of zeta zeros accounted for by distinct points on the critical line as the height grows.

83.625%

The lower bound

One location, one count.

The numerator counts each distinct zero on the line once. The denominator counts every nontrivial zero with its multiplicity. A double zero contributes one above the fraction bar and two below it.

Inside the proof

Two theorems,
one corollary.

The plot shows ten examples. The proof covers arbitrarily large heights.

Nd

Count distinct zeros

Alpöge and Furman prove a lower bound for distinct zeros anywhere in the critical strip. Their constant is slightly larger than 669/800.

½

Place them on the line

Alpöge’s released Lean theorem places every nontrivial zero at real part ½. The distinct count in the strip therefore equals the distinct count on the line in each window.

κ

Check both windows

Lean checks the exact fraction 669/800 from height T to 2T and from 0 to T. For every ε > 0, both proportions are at least 669/800 − ε at all sufficiently large heights.
